Equity is the third pillar of the accounting equation, along with assets and liabilities.


> [!IMPORTANT] Definition 1
> Equity is the residual value of an entity's assets after deducting all it's liabilities.


> [!NOTE] Equation
> **Equity = Assets - Liabilities**

Equity is calculated as assets minus liabilities. In other words, equity is the net assets of a business.

> [!IMPORTANT] Definition 2
> Equity represents the net funds invested into a business by its owners.

##### Components

Understanding the structure of a business can impact the concept of equity. The structure of a business influences the terminology used to describe equity.

![[Equity (1).png]]

Equity is essentially made up of three things:
1. **Capital Contributions**: These are the funds injected into the business at the initial stage of the business before turning profitable.

> [!NOTE] Equation
> Equity consists of capital contributions and retained earnings.
> **Equity = Capital Contributions + Retained Earnings** 

2. **Retained Earnings**: 
	- Retained earnings are accumulated profits held for future use. They can be reinvested in the business or used for personal expenses.
	- Retained earnings consist of accumulated profits from revenues less expenses (*withdrawals*), as shown in the [[Income Statement]] income statement.


> [!NOTE] Equations
> - **Retained Earning = Accumulated Profit - Withdrawals**
> - **Accumulated Profit = Revenue + Expenses**
