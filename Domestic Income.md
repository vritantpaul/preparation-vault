> [!QUESTION]
> Domestic income has two components: first, income from domestic product accruing to private sector; and second, income from domestic product accruing to public sector. The latter has two components: income from property and entrepreneurship accruing to government administrative departments and savings of non-departmental enterprises. Explain the following concepts to me.

## 1️⃣ Income from domestic product accruing to the **private sector**

This is the **largest and most intuitive part** of domestic income.

It includes **factor incomes earned by private individuals and private enterprises** from production within the domestic territory.

**Examples:**

- Wages and salaries earned by workers in private firms
    
- Rent earned by private landlords
    
- Interest earned by private lenders
    
- Profits earned by private companies and entrepreneurs
    
- Mixed income of self-employed persons
    

👉 In short:

> **Private sector income = factor incomes earned by private households and firms from domestic production.**

---

## 2️⃣ Income from domestic product accruing to the **public sector**

This is where confusion usually starts.

The **government also earns income** from production activities carried out within the economy. This income is **not tax revenue** — taxes are **not factor income**.

Public sector income has **two distinct components**:

---

### (a) **Income from property and entrepreneurship accruing to government administrative departments**

Government administrative departments (railways, postal services, defense workshops, public hospitals, etc.) may:

- Own **property** (land, buildings)
    
- Undertake **entrepreneurial activities** (public production units)
    

The income they earn includes:

- Rent from government-owned property
    
- Interest earned on government lending
    
- Profits (or surplus) from government-run enterprises
    

👉 This income arises because the government acts as a **factor owner**, not as a tax collector.

**Key point:**

> This is **factor income of the government**, so it is included in **domestic income**.

---

### (b) **Savings of non-departmental enterprises**

Non-departmental enterprises = **public sector enterprises** that function like companies.

**Examples:**

- Public sector undertakings (PSUs)
    
- Government companies
    
- Statutory corporations
    

They earn profits just like private firms. These profits are used in three ways:

1. **Dividends** paid to government
    
2. **Corporate taxes** paid to government
    
3. **Savings (retained earnings)**
    

The **savings (retained profits)** part is what this component refers to.

👉 These savings represent:

> **Undistributed profit earned from domestic production**, hence part of domestic income.

---

## How everything fits together (big picture)

**Domestic Income =**

- Income accruing to **private sector**
    
- - Income accruing to **public sector**, which consists of:
        
    
    - Income from property & entrepreneurship of government departments
        
    - Savings of non-departmental (public) enterprises
        

---

## One-line intuition (very important)

> Domestic income includes **all factor incomes generated within the country**, whether earned by **private agents or the government**.
