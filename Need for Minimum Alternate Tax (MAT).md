# 1. Two different ways profits appear

A company reports profit in two different frameworks:

1. Book Profit → Profit shown in the company’s financial statements (as per the Companies Act).


2. Taxable Income → Profit calculated under the Income Tax Act after deductions, exemptions, depreciation, etc.



Because the Income Tax Act allows many deductions, taxable income can become much lower than the actual book profit.


---

# 2. The problem the government noticed

Some companies were showing large profits in their financial statements (to impress investors) but very small taxable income (to reduce tax liability).

So they were:

reporting high profits to shareholders

paying very little tax to the government


These were called “zero tax companies.”


---

# 3. Example without MAT

Suppose a company reports:

Book Profit (as per Companies Act)
₹100 crore

But under the Income Tax Act it claims deductions like:

accelerated depreciation

tax exemptions

investment deductions


So its Taxable Income becomes:

₹10 crore

Corporate tax rate (assume): 25%

Tax payable = 25% × 10 crore = ₹2.5 crore

Even though the company actually earned ₹100 crore, it only pays ₹2.5 crore tax.


---

# 4. Introduction of MAT

To prevent this, the government introduced Minimum Alternate Tax (MAT).

MAT says:

> If a company’s normal tax is very low, it must pay a minimum tax on its book profit.



MAT rate (approx): 15% of book profit


---

# 5. Example with MAT

Same company:

Book Profit = ₹100 crore

Step 1 — Calculate normal corporate tax

Taxable income = ₹10 crore

Corporate tax =
25% × 10 crore = ₹2.5 crore

Step 2 — Calculate MAT

MAT =
15% × 100 crore = ₹15 crore

Step 3 — Compare both

Method	Tax

Corporate Tax	₹2.5 crore
MAT	₹15 crore


The rule says:

> Company must pay whichever is higher.



So the company pays ₹15 crore (MAT).


---

# 6. Final idea (the intuition)

MAT ensures:

> If a company is profitable on paper, it cannot avoid paying a minimum level of tax by using deductions.



So the rule becomes:

Tax payable = Higher of

Corporate tax (based on taxable income)

MAT (based on book profit)
