- Assets can be broadly divided into current (<= 1 year), and non-current (> 1 year) assets.
- Non-current asset is further divided into, long-term investments, tangible and non-tangible assets.
- Tangible asset is also known as fixed asset. Meaning to be a fixed asset, an asset has to be:
	1. Tangible
	2. Has a useful economic life of more than a year.
- Fixed asset is an accounting concept.
---
- Real asset is an economic concept.
- For something to be a real asset it has to be tangible. That's the only property an asset needs to have to be considered a real asset.
- In other words, it includes both current and non-current assets.
---
- Real capital is an economic concept.
- Real capital consists of those real assets which are used in the production of goods and services.
- In other words, real capital ⊂ real asset.
- Real capital is synonymous with capital goods. Which is one of the two types of final goods.
- Example of real assets which are not real capital: unused land and gold bullion.
- Assets which "enable" the production process are fixed assets. Therefore, fixed assets are part of real capital.
- <s>Only raw materials which are directly used in the production process comes under real capital.</s> Inventory is included in real capital.
- Inventory consists of raw materials, Work in Progress (WIP), and finished goods.
---
- Capital stock is aggregate value of real capital in an economy.
---
- Investment, broadly speaking, is the act of allocating resources (typically money), with the expectation of generating profit.
- In macroeconomics, when we say investment we mean capital formation which is the addition to the capital stock of an economy.
- Investment is either synonymous with gross investment or is a umbrella term including gross and net investment. The latter usage is only when a distinction needs to be made.
- Gross investment includes depreciation. Net investment doesn't.
