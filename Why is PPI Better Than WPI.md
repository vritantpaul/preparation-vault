Think of a product moving through stages of production. The problem with WPI is that the same price increase can get counted multiple times as the product moves through stages.

Example

Suppose cotton becomes more expensive.

Stage 1: Raw cotton → sold to textile mill

Price increases from ₹100 → ₹120


Stage 2: Textile mill → makes yarn

Yarn price becomes ₹200 → ₹230


Stage 3: Garment manufacturer → makes shirt

Shirt price becomes ₹500 → ₹560


What happens in WPI

WPI measures prices of goods at different wholesale stages.
So the cotton price increase gets reflected:

Once in raw cotton price

Again in yarn price (because cotton is an input)

Again in shirt price (because yarn is an input)


👉 The same original cost increase (cotton) influences multiple product prices, so inflation may appear overstated due to multiple counting.

What PPI does differently

Producer Price Index (PPI) measures prices stage-wise or on a value-added basis.

Instead of counting the whole price each time, it looks at the additional value created at each stage.

Example:

Stage	Total Price	Value Added

Cotton farmer	₹120	₹120
Textile mill	₹230	₹110
Garment maker	₹560	₹330


PPI focuses on value added at each stage, avoiding repeated counting of the same input cost.

✅ Result: Inflation measurement becomes cleaner and more accurate.

One-line intuition (good for exams)

WPI counts the full price at every stage, while PPI tracks only the value added at each stage, preventing multiple counting of the same input cost.
