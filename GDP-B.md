GDP-B, which stands for Gross Domestic Product - Benefits, is a proposed new measure designed to provide a more comprehensive picture of economic welfare and growth, particularly in the context of a digital economy. It aims to address the limitations of traditional GDP, which was developed in the 1930s and may not fully capture the value of modern economic activities and societal well-being.

Here's a breakdown of what GDP-B entails:

- Focus on Benefits, Not Just Price: Unlike GDP, which measures the monetary value of goods and services, GDP-B focuses on how much consumers benefit from these offerings. It's rooted in the economic principle that changes in well-being stem from changes in economic surplus, rather than just the money spent.

- Addressing GDP's Shortcomings:
	- Excludes Non-Market Activities: Traditional GDP doesn't account for valuable activities like volunteering, work done at home (e.g., caring for children or the elderly), or the value derived from things like environmental quality, personal health, or cultural amenities.
	- Includes "Bads" as "Goods": Events like natural disasters or accidents, which decrease welfare, can actually increase GDP due to spending on repairs and clean-up. GDP-B aims to correct this by looking at welfare more broadly.
	- Digital Economy: GDP struggles to measure the value of intangible and digital services that characterize the modern economy.


- Key Components of GDP-B Measurement:
	- Consumer Surplus: This measures the welfare people gain from consuming products and services, going beyond just the price paid. It includes everyday purchases like coffee and more significant ones like a new TV.
	- Free Digital Goods: GDP-B aims to incorporate the value people derive from free digital services like Google Maps or Instagram, which are not captured by traditional GDP.
	- Sources of Welfare: It acknowledges and seeks to measure the value from other crucial aspects of life, such as improved health, cleaner air, reduced pollution, and household production.
- Broader Insights: GDP-B can offer new insights into:
	- Digital Products: Understanding which digital tools provide value and how this varies across different groups and regions.
	- Environment: Gauging public preferences for environmental protection and how these change over time or in response to events.
	- Health: Recognizing the significant welfare gains from lower mortality and morbidity, which are often overlooked by national statistics.
	- Inequality: Providing a more equitable measure of value creation, as traditional GDP is biased towards those with higher purchasing power.
	- Household Production: Acknowledging the economic value of activities within households, which are often disproportionately undertaken by women and thus underrepresented in GDP.


- Methodology: GDP-B utilizes advances in massive online experiments to empirically measure concepts like consumer surplus, which were previously considered too challenging to quantify.

- Goal: The ultimate vision is for GDP-B to become a key indicator for policymakers, regulators, and investors, enabling better decision-making to maximize the well-being of current and future generations.

The GDP-B project is supported by institutions like the National Science Foundation and involves researchers from various academic institutions.
