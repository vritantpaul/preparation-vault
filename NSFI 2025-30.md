START
Basic++
What are the <i>demand side impediments</i> to financial inclusion?
Back: 
1. Unmet credit needs despite demand.
2. Information asymmetry on <i>availability</i>, <i>cost</i>, and <i>usage of services</i>.
3. Language and literacy barriers.
4. Low awareness of insurance/pension needs and schemes.*
5. Irregular income limiting active use despite high account ownership.


Footnotes: Among the respondents who did not have any insurance/pension product, the awareness about the need for insurance and pension products was found to be low. In general, awareness about schemes like PMSBY, PMJJBY, PMFBY, and APY ranged between<b> 30-35%</b>.
Source: [[NSFI 2025-30.pdf]]
Tags: B&F
<!--ID: 1769492989670-->
END

START
Basic++
What are the <i>supply side impediments</i> to financial inclusion?
Back: 

1. Restricted range of services at BC outlets → uneven access.
2. Lack of prescribed minimum standards → poor standardisation and monitoring.
3. Non-transparent fees and unauthorized charges.*
4. Low representation of women BC agents → reduced outreach and inclusivity.


Footnotes: Most BCs did not display service fees, and concerns existed about unauthorized or unaccounted upfront charges in addition to bank-built transaction charges.
Source: [[NSFI 2025-30.pdf]]

## Way Forward

Financial inclusion policies duly integrated with financial education, customer protection, skill development, livelihood support, entrepreneurship, and market linkages, with a multi-stakeholder approach, can be enablers of desired outcomes reflected in an improved state of financial safety, financial security, financial resilience, and financial discipline, in turn contributing towards wellbeing of people. Hence, adoption of a holistic and synergistic approach to financial inclusion is the way forward.
