## Revenue Receipt

START
Basic++
What are the two conditions for a receipt to be a revenue receipt?
Back: 
A receipt is a revenue receipt, if it satisfies the following two essential conditions:

<details>
	<summary>1. <b>The receipt must not create a liability for the government.</b></summary>	
	Taxes levied by the government are revenue receipts as they do not create any liability. However, any amount, borrowed by the government, is not a revenue receipt as it causes an increase in the liability in terms of repayment of borrowings.
</details>
<details>
	<summary>2. <b>The receipt must not cause decrease in the assets.</b></summary>	
	Receipts from sale of shares of a public enterprise is not a revenue receipt as it leads to a reduction in assets of the government.
</details>

Footnotes:
Source: Ch 10: Government Budget and the Economy (Introductory Macroeconomics for Class XII by Sandeep Garg)

Basic++
What is a proportional tax system?
Back: A system in which the tax rate remains constant with increase or decrease in the income.
Footnotes: Example: If tax rate is 10% and the annual income of a person is ₹ 1,00,000, then he will have to pay 10,000 per year as tax. If income rises to ₹ 1,25,000 per annum, then the tax liability will rise to ₹ 12,500 per year. In this case, burden of tax is more on the poor section as compared to rich section.
Source: Ch 10: Government Budget and the Economy (Introductory Macroeconomics for Class XII by Sandeep Garg)

	<summary>^ <b>How Firms And Companies are Treated Differently Than the Other Four</b></summary>	
	Firms and companies are "persons" under the Income-tax Act (so they appear in the legal definition). </details>

Why was there a need for Minimum Alternate Tax (MAT)?
Back: Generally, income tax is computed as per the provisions of the IT Act. However, companies compute their book profit as per provisions of Companies (Amendment) Act, 2019, and as IT Act allows several kinds of exemptions, depreciations and deductions from gross income or book profit, companies in many instances can show their taxable income as very less, on which the amount of tax comes out to be very low. Therefore, for these zero taxes or very less tax companies, MAT was introduced. Now, a company must pay at least either corporate tax or MAT, whichever is higher.

What is meant by "inverted duty structure" in the GST regime?
Back: A situation where the rate of tax on inputs purchased (i.e. GST rate paid on inputs received) is more than the rate of tax (i.e. GST rate payable) on output.
Footnotes: For example, non-woven fabric is used as the raw material for fabric bags. Now, non-woven fabrics are taxed at 12% and fabric bags are taxed at 5%. So, in this scenario, ITC always gets accumulated at the fabric bags manufacturer's end. Finally, it leads to refund of such unused ITC.^ 
\***
This most often results in cash flow problems for industry as working capitals remained underutilised or blocked due to the higher amount of duty paid on inputs.

## Capital Receipt

START
Basic++
What are the two conditions for a receipt to be a capital receipt?
Back: 
A receipt is a capital receipt if it satisfies <u>any one</u> of the two conditions:

<details>
	<summary>1. <b>The receipts must create a liability for the government.</b></summary>	
	For example, Borrowings are capital receipts as they lead to an increase in the liability of the government. However, tax received is not a capital receipt as it does not result in creation of any liability.
</details>
<details>
	<summary>2. <b>The receipts must cause a decrease in the assets.</b></summary>	
	For example, receipts from sale of shares of public enterprise is a capital receipt as it leads to reduction in assets of the government.
</details>

Footnotes:
Source: Ch 10: Government Budget and the Economy (Introductory Macroeconomics for Class XII by Sandeep Garg)
