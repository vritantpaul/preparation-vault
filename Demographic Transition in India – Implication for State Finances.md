Basic++
What is the impact of population age structure on revenue?
Back: 
1. In the youthful States, a larger workforce, if employed effectively, can create a larger tax base, boosting both direct and indirect tax receipts.
2. Increased urbanisation can further boost tax receipt in these States by shifting away from hard-to-tax sectors like agriculture.
3. In contrast, in the ageing States, a gradually shrinking labour force will lower the long-term growth rate and thus partly erode the tax base, particularly, for personal income taxes.

Footnotes:
Source: [[State Finances - A Study of Budgets of 2025-26.pdf]]

START
Basic++
How did East Asian countries tackled the ageing problem and managed to enjoy a second and even third wave of demographic dividend? 
Back: 
1. Raised retirement age (Japan & South Korea).
2. Introduced defined contribution scheme* (Japan, Hong Kong and Singapore).
3. Established long-term insurance (Japan & South Korea).
4. Cut replacement rates^ (South Korea).
5. Switched from wage-based to price-based indexation (Japan & South Korea).
6. Expanded pension coverage (China).

Footnotes: 
<details>
	<summary><b>Defined Contribution Scheme</b></summary>
	A retirement plan where employee and employer contributions are fixed, but the final retirement benefit depends on investment performance.	
</details>

\***
^ <details>
	<summary><b>Income Replacement Rate</b></summary>	
	Percentage of pre-retirement income needed to maintain a similar standard of living after stopping work. After two consecutive reforms, South Korea managed to bring down the replacement rate from 70% to 50%.
</details>

\***
$ <details>
	<summary><b>Shifting From Wage-Based to Price-Based Indexation</b></summary>	
	 It involves changing the benchmark used to adjust financial contracts, wages, or tax brackets from a measure of prevailing wage growth to a measure of inflation (cost of living), such as the Consumer Price Index (CPI).
</details>

Source: [[State Finances - A Study of Budgets of 2025-26.pdf]]
