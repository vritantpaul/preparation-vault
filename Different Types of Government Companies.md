These four categories represent distinct ways the Indian government organizes administration, regulation, and public enterprise, ranging from direct ministerial control to independent corporate structures.

| Dimension                 | Government Administrative Department                                            | Statutory Body / Corporation                                          | Public Sector Undertaking (PSU)                                        | Non-PSU Government Company                                                          |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Legal Origin**          | Executive order under the Constitution (Allocation of Business Rules).          | Special Act of Parliament or a State Legislature.                     | Companies Act, 2013 (51%+ govt shares) or a special statute.           | Companies Act, 2013 (51%+ govt shares), often under **Section 8** (not-for-profit). |
| **Separate Legal Entity** | No. It is a direct limb of the government.                                      | Yes. Created as an autonomous body by its parent Act.                 | Yes. Incorporated as a separate corporate entity.                      | Yes. Incorporated as a separate corporate entity.                                   |
| **Primary Purpose**       | Core governance, policy making, public administration, and sovereign functions. | Sector regulation, oversight, or specialized public service delivery. | Commercial production, banking, or infrastructure services for profit. | Non-commercial welfare, skill development, or policy promotion.                     |
| **Funding & Budget**      | Directly funded through the Consolidated Fund of India via annual budgets.      | Government grants, statutory levies, or user fees.                    | Self-sustaining commercial revenue, market borrowing, and equity.      | Government grants, CSR contributions, and nominal project fees.                     |
| **Operational Autonomy**  | Low. Bound by strict civil service rules, GFR, and ministerial approval.        | Moderate to High. Governed by its specific enabling Act.              | High. Managed by a Board of Directors (especially Maharatna/Navratna). | High. Corporate agility for hiring and public-private partnerships.                 |
| **Staff Status**          | Civil servants and government employees.                                        | Employees of the specific statutory body (not civil servants).        | Corporate PSU employees governed by company HR rules.                  | Corporate/contractual staff governed by company HR rules.                           |
| **Examples**              | Indian Railways, India Post, Ministry of Defence                                | SEBI, RBI, NHRC, Food Corporation of India (FCI)                      | ONGC, NTPC, BHEL, SBI, IRCTC                                           | Digital India Corporation (DIC), Invest India, NSDC                                 |

> [!IMPORTANT] Quick Rule of Thumb
> - No separate legal existence: It is a Government Department.
> - Created by its own specific law: It is a Statutory Body / Corporation.
> - Registered under the Companies Act to make profit: It is a PSU.
> - Registered under the Companies Act for non-profit public goals: It is a Non-PSU Government Company.
>
