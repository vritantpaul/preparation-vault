**Yes—your numbers and logic are 100% spot-on.** Every single rupee balances across all three sectors.

Here is how your exact ₹10 lakh example closes the loop in National Income accounting:

## 1. Cash Flows (What Actually Moves)

- **Firm → Bank:** Pays ₹1,00,000 total interest (10% of ₹10 lakh).
- **Bank → Household:** Pays ₹40,000 actual interest (4% of ₹10 lakh).
- **Bank Keeps:** **₹60,000** (the net interest spread).

## 2. National Income Accounting Breakdown (How It Is Recorded)

Using the **6% reference rate** (₹60,000 pure interest):

- **Firm's Books:**
    - Pays **₹60,000** as _Pure Interest_ (factor payment).
    - Pays **₹40,000** as _FISIM_ (treated like buying a service/intermediate input, so it is deducted from the Firm's Value Added).
- **Household's Books:**
    - Earns **₹60,000** as _Pure Interest Income_ (even though they only received ₹40,000 in cash).
    - Spends **₹20,000** on _FISIM_ (treated as Final Consumption Expenditure on banking services, leaving ₹40,000 net cash).
- **Bank's Books:**
    - **Output / Revenue (Total FISIM):** ₹40,000 (from firm) + ₹20,000 (from household) = **₹60,000**.
    - **Pure Interest Pass-Through:** Receives ₹60,000 pure interest from the firm and pays ₹60,000 pure interest to the household (net = **₹0**).

## 3. Final Impact on GDP / National Income

- **Value Added Method:** Bank's output (**₹60,000**) minus Firm's intermediate consumption (**₹40,000**) = **+₹20,000** net addition to GDP (which equals the household's final consumption of banking services!).
- **Income Method:** Household's pure interest (**₹60,000**) + Bank's wages/profit from its ₹60,000 revenue (**₹60,000**) − Firm's reduced profit (**₹1,00,000**) = **+₹20,000** net addition to National Income.
