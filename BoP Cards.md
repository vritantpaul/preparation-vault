Basic++
What are Autonomous Items?
Back: Those international economic transactions, which take place <u>due to some economic motive such as profit maximisation</u>\*.
Footnotes: Autonomous transactions are independent of the state of BoP account. In other words, people and firms make these transactions without caring whether the country has a BoP surplus or deficit.
\***
Examples:
- On the <b>current account</b>, merchandise exports and imports of goods are autonomous transactions.
- On the <b>capital account</b>, receipts and repayments of long-term loans by private individuals are autonomous transactions.

Source: Ch 12: Balance of Payments (Introductory Macroeconomics for Class XII by Sandeep Garg)
Reverse:

What should be considered as FDI as per Mayaram Panel's recommendation?
Back: 
1. Any foreign investment equal to or beyond >= 10% stake in <u>post-issue paid-up equity capital on a fully diluted basis</u>* in a listed company.
2. Any investment in an unlisted entity (even if it is only 1 or 2% of paid-up capital).

Footnotes: Total number of shares the company would have after issuing new shares AND after converting all possible future shares into equity.

Let’s say a company has:

1,000 existing shares

Issues 200 new shares → now 1,200 (post-issue)

Plus:
300 ESOPs
500 convertible debentures

On a fully diluted basis:
Total shares = 1000 + 200 + 300 + 500 = 2,000 shares

What is the Twin Deficit Hypothesis?
Back: Economies that have <u>both a fiscal deficit and current account deficit</u> are referred to as having '<b>twin deficits</b>'. As per twin deficit hypothesis, some economists believe that <u>a large budget deficit is correlated to a large current account deficit</u>.*
Footnotes: For example - the United States and India have experienced both the deficits year after year.
