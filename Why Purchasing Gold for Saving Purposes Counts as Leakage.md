> [!QUESTION]
> So, buying gold bars won't be counted under PFCE, because it is bought with the purpose of saving, instead of consumption. And that is why we must treat it as leakage?


**Yes, that is completely spot on.** In National Income Accounting (such as India's MoSPI framework and the UN System of National Accounts), **gold bars and coins bought by households are excluded from PFCE** because they are treated as a **store of value (saving/valuables)** rather than consumption, which directly aligns with why they are treated as a **leakage** from the circular flow of income.

1. Why Gold Bars Are Excluded from PFCE

- **Consumption vs. Store of Value:** Private Final Consumption Expenditure (PFCE) measures goods and services bought to satisfy current human wants (like food, clothes, healthcare, or wearing jewellery). Gold bars and coins do not get "used up" or provide utility through wear; they are bought purely to hold wealth over time.
- **How GDP Accounts for Gold Bars:** Under the UN System of National Accounts (SNA 2008) used by India, pure gold bullion bought by households is classified under **"Acquisitions less disposals of valuables"** (a sub-component of **Gross Capital Formation / Investment**, not PFCE). Because it is not counted in `C` (PFCE), buying a gold bar does not boost consumer demand in GDP.

2. Why This Makes It a Leakage

- **Saving without Financial Intermediation:** In the circular flow formula (`Leakages = S + T + M`), any income not spent on `C` (PFCE) is classified as **Saving (S)**—which is by definition a leakage from the consumption stream.
- **Unproductive Leakage:** Normally, when you put savings (`S`) into a bank or mutual fund, the financial system converts that leakage back into an **injection** (`I` = productive business investment like factories, machinery, and loans). When you buy a gold bar instead, your saving gets parked in an **idle "valuable"** that neither counts as consumption (`C`) nor funds business expansion (`I`), leaving it as a pure leakage from active economic circulation.

Quick Comparison in National Accounting

|Purchase Type|National Accounts Category|Circular Flow Role|
|---|---|---|
|**Gold Jewellery**|**PFCE (`C`)** (treated as personal consumption of ornaments)|**Consumption** (domestic making/margin circulates; imported bullion leaks via `M`)|
|**Gold Bars / Coins**|**Valuables (`Gross Capital Formation`)** (excluded from PFCE)|**Leakage (`S` / Hoarding)** (withdrawn from consumption without entering banking credit)|
