> [[Balance Sheet]]

Assets are one of the three pillars of the accounting equation, along with liabilities and equity.

> [!NOTE] Equation
> Assets = Liabilities + Equity

> [!IMPORTANT] Definition
> Assets are <i><u>probable</u></i> <i><u>future economic benefits</u></i> <i><u>obtained or controlled</u></i> by a particular entity as a result of past transactions or events.

##### Breakdown
1. **Probable**
	- The word 'probable' implies uncertainty, emphasizing the need to question estimates and account for potential losses.
	- Accountants make allowances for doubtful debts, estimating potential unrecovered amounts and expenses.<br><br>

2. **Future economic benefits**
	- Future economic benefits are the value assets bring directly or indirectly to you or your business.
	- Valuing assets based on future economic benefits involves estimating their *useful economic life[^1]* for depreciation purposes.<br><br>

3. **Obtained or controlled** 
	- *Substance over form principle* prioritizes the economic substance of transactions over their legal form when preparing financial statements.
	- Even if a business doesn't legally own a building, it can still be accounted as an asset based on its economic reality and implications for accounting and tax treatment.

[^1]: How long an asset will remain useful to you.
##### [[Balance Sheet]]

> [!IMPORTANT] Definition
> A balance sheet is a snapshot of a business's assets, liabilities, and equity at a specific point in time.

##### Classification of Assets
1. **Current Assets**: Current assets are those that can be converted into cash within a short period of time, usually within a year.
	Most common types of current assets:
	1. *Cash*
	2. *Accounts Receivable*
	3. *Inventory*
	4. *Prepayment*: Paid in advanced.
	5. *Short-term Investment*: Short-term investments can be considered as current assets if sold within a year.<br><br>

2. **Non-current Assets**: Non-current assets are long-term assets that are used in operations to generate profits, and that can't easily be converted into cash. Non-current assets include:
	1. *Long-term Investments*
	2. *Tangible assets* (physical presence): Examples: Land & Buildings, or **PPE** (Property, Plant and Equipment).
	3. *Intangible assets*: 
		- Intangible assets like intellectual property, patent, royalty rights, trademarks, and copyright provide future economic benefits.
		- Valuing intangible assets can be challenging, leading most businesses to not capitalize internally generated intangible assets.
		- *Goodwill* is a type of intangible asset.
			- Goodwill is a premium paid for brand value and future earnings.
			- Goodwill is the extra amount paid for a company over its net assets' fair value.
			- Example: Facebook paid a premium for Instagram in 2012, mainly for its brand and potential.
